# Wedding Videography Packages in Los Angeles — Coverage, Cost, and What You Actually Get

Wedding videography packages in Los Angeles start at $3,959 for ceremony and toasts coverage with a single shooter, and range to $14,299+ for full-day photo and video combined packages with two video operators, two photographers, and delivery within 10 weeks. Most couples booking at venues like Calamigos Ranch, Hotel Bel-Air, or Bel-Air Bay Club invest between $7,500 and $10,000 for a package that covers 8–10 hours of the day with a 2-shooter video team, a 3–5 minute highlight reel, and a full ceremony edit.

The pricing structure reflects three variables: **coverage hours** (how much of the day you want filmed), **shooter count** (one operator versus a two-camera setup that captures ceremony wide and close simultaneously), and **deliverables** (highlight reel only, or highlight reel plus full ceremony, toasts, first dance, and raw footage archive). A fourth variable—whether you book photo and video together—typically reduces the combined cost by 15–20% compared to hiring separately.

## What Drives Package Price in the Los Angeles Market

![White convertible car on a busy Los Angeles street with a city skyline in the background.](/blog-images/wedding-videography-packages-los-angeles/section-2-33fef62aeaa4.jpg)

*Photo by RITESH SINGH on Pexels*


Three cost factors separate a $4,000 package from a $14,000 package: labor hours, team size, and post-production complexity.

### 1. Coverage Hours and Team Size

A single videographer working 6 hours bills approximately $600–$700 per hour when amortized across the package (gear, insurance, editing, delivery infrastructure included). A 2-shooter team working 10 hours represents 20 labor-hours of on-site capture, plus 40–60 hours of editing, color correction, audio mixing, and export—roughly 60–80 total hours per wedding. At luxury-wedding labor rates in Los Angeles, that accounts for $6,000–$7,500 of the package price before business overhead.

### 2. Deliverables and Edit Complexity

A 3-minute highlight reel requires 8–12 hours of editing: footage review, selects assembly, music licensing and beat-sync, color grade pass, audio mix (ceremony vows, toasts, ambient sound), and export. A full ceremony edit—20 to 35 minutes uncut—adds another 6–8 hours: multicam sync, audio cleanup for officiant and lapel mics, color matching across two cameras, and chapter markers. Toast edits and full reception coverage add another 4–6 hours per deliverable.

Couples who want raw footage archives (every clip from every camera, unedited) add $800–$1,200 to the package price. The archive is delivered on a USB drive; it typically contains 120–200 GB of 4K files, organized by camera and time block.

### 3. Travel and Multi-Day Logistics

Weddings at Ojai Valley Inn, San Ysidro Ranch, or Terranea Resort require 2–3 hours of round-trip travel from Los Angeles. Destination weddings—Napa, Carmel, Italy, France—add travel days, hotel nights, and gear shipping. A two-day Napa wedding (rehearsal dinner film + wedding day) typically adds $2,500–$4,000 to the base package for travel, lodging, per diem, and the second day of coverage.

Italy and France destination packages start at $12,000 and include round-trip airfare for two crew members, 3–5 nights of lodging, carnets for gear customs clearance, and liability insurance that covers EU shoots. The per-wedding cost reflects the fact that we block 6–8 days for a single international wedding (travel days, site scout, shoot days, return).

## How to Choose the Right Package for Your Venue and Day

![Beautiful outdoor wedding ceremony setting with white and pastel-colored decor and floral arrangements.](/blog-images/wedding-videography-packages-los-angeles/section-3-62f03b7275ec.jpg)

*Photo by NUDE Nahum on Pexels*


The package you need is determined by three factors: your venue's scene structure, your coverage priorities, and whether you care more about the highlight reel or the ceremony archive.

### Venue Scene Structure

**Single-location urban venues** (Hotel Bel-Air, Beverly Hills Hotel, Bel-Air Bay Club) work with 1-shooter essential coverage if the ceremony, cocktail hour, and reception all happen within 200 feet. The shooter moves between scenes without missing key moments.

**Multi-location resort and ranch venues** (Calamigos Ranch, Ojai Valley Inn, Saddlerock Ranch, Rancho Valencia) require 2-shooter full-day coverage. The ceremony oak grove is a 5-minute walk from the barn; cocktail hour happens on a lawn the video team cannot see from the reception setup. Two shooters ensure that no transition moment—guests moving from ceremony to cocktails, the couple's private golden-hour portrait window—goes unrecorded.

**Destination and estate venues** (San Ysidro Ranch, Montage Laguna Beach, Auberge du Soleil) typically require the photo + video combined package. The day spans 10–12 hours, includes a first look in one garden and a ceremony in another, and the planner has built 90 minutes of golden-hour couple and family portraits into the timeline. A unified crew keeps the timeline on pace; separate photo and video vendors create coordination friction.

### Coverage Priorities — Highlight Reel vs Full Ceremony

If you will rewatch the **highlight reel** dozens of times and the full ceremony once, book the essential or full-day package with the highlight reel as the primary deliverable. If your parents, grandparents, or out-of-town guests want to experience the **full ceremony as it happened**—including the 4-minute reading, the 8-minute homily, and the uncut vow exchange—add the full ceremony edit to your package.

Couples who prioritize the cinematic edit over the documentary archive typically choose the 3–5 minute highlight reel and skip the full ceremony. Couples planning religious or interfaith ceremonies with significant liturgical content, or couples whose vows are deeply personal and were written over months, typically add the full ceremony deliverable.

### The First Look Decision

If your timeline includes a first look—the couple sees each other 60–90 minutes before the ceremony, privately or with only the photographer and videographer present—you benefit from 2-shooter coverage. One shooter captures the bride's approach (wide, steady); the second captures the groom's reaction (close, handheld). The first look is a 90-second moment that plays as the emotional centerpiece of the highlight reel; single-shooter coverage can document it, but cannot hold both angles simultaneously.

Planners at Calamigos Ranch and Hummingbird Nest Ranch build the first look into the timeline specifically because it creates a private, repeatable moment—if the groom's reaction is too quiet on the first take, the couple can reset and try again, which is impossible during the processional.

## How Booking Photo and Video Together Changes the Timeline and the Price

When a couple books a [Photo + Video Combined Package](https://lifestory.film/services/photo-video-combined-package/) from a single vendor, three workflow efficiencies reduce cost and improve the day-of experience.

### 1. Unified Timeline Coordination

One team manages the entire visual timeline. The photographer and videographer pre-walk the venue together, decide on first-look location and ceremony angles, and agree on portrait-session pacing before the wedding day. There is no coordination call between two separate vendors, no conflicting lighting preferences, no double-booking of the couple's golden-hour window.

### 2. Shared Lighting and Setup

During family formals and couple portraits, the photography lighting setup (off-camera flash or continuous LED panel) also serves the videographer. At sunset on the Bel-Air Bay Club terrace, one lighting setup captures both stills and motion; separate vendors would each bring their own lighting, doubling setup time and adding visual clutter to the background of frames.

### 3. Reduced Travel and Logistics Overhead

One crew travels to the venue in one vehicle, checks in as a single vendor team, and shares the same load-in and setup time block. For Ojai Valley Inn and San Ysidro Ranch resort weddings, this eliminates the parking coordination, vendor meal coordination, and timeline conflict that arises when photo and video arrive separately.

The cost savings—$1,800 to $2,500—comes from the vendor's side (shared travel, shared assistant labor, shared timeline-coordination overhead) and is passed to the couple as a package discount.

## Timing — When to Book and What the Booking Process Looks Like

Wedding videography in Los Angeles books 10–14 months in advance for June, September, and October weddings. January through March and November weddings book 6–10 months out. Destination weddings in Italy and France book 12–18 months in advance due to travel logistics and limited annual availability (LifeStory.Film accepts 8–10 international weddings per year).

### Booking Process

1. **Inquiry and initial call** — 20–30 minute video call to discuss your venue, timeline, coverage priorities, and which package aligns with your day. We ask about the ceremony structure (religious, secular, interfaith), whether you're planning a first look, and what your coverage end-point is (last dance, sparkler exit, or after-party).

2. **Proposal and contract** — Custom proposal with package details, deliverables list, and timeline. Contract includes payment schedule (50% deposit to book the date, 50% due 2 weeks before the wedding), cancellation terms, and deliverable specifications.

3. **Pre-wedding timeline call** — 4–6 weeks before the wedding, a 45-minute call with the couple and planner to finalize the shot list, confirm ceremony start time and processional order, review first-look logistics, identify family formal groupings, and confirm the golden-hour portrait window.

4. **Wedding day** — The video team arrives 60–90 minutes before getting-ready coverage begins for setup, venue walk-through, and coordination with the photographer, planner, and venue staff.

5. **Delivery** — Highlight reel delivered in 10–12 weeks; full ceremony edit within 14 weeks; full toast and dance edits within 16 weeks. All films deliver via private Vimeo link (streaming, password-protected) plus downloadable 4K file. USB drive shipped on request.
