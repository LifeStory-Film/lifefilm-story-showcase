# Destination Wedding Videographer — Italy, France, Japan, and Worldwide Coverage

A destination wedding videographer travels with the coverage plan, the backup gear, and the location knowledge required to film weddings at Borgo Egnazia, Le Sirenuse, Aman Tokyo, and Hotel du Cap-Eden-Roc — venues where the couple's guest count often reaches 80 to 150, where multi-day timelines span welcome dinners and post-wedding brunches, and where the couple expects a 2-shooter video team with international experience and dual-camera 4K coverage from ceremony through reception.

LifeStory.Film documents destination weddings across Italy, France, Japan, Mexico, and the Caribbean, with a service model built for couples who plan weddings at international venues where light, language, and logistics differ from California coverage but where editorial quality and deliverable timelines remain the same.

## Why Destination Weddings Require a Different Coverage Model Than Regional Work

![A happy bride and bridesmaids posing outdoors for a wedding photo in León de los Aldama.](/blog-images/destination-wedding-videographer/section-1-9f3c07b517ed.jpg)

*Photo by Luis Felipe Pérez on Pexels*


A destination wedding videographer accounts for variables that do not appear on a California timeline — international power standards, local vendor coordination in multiple languages, customs documentation for camera gear, backup equipment redundancy when rental houses are unavailable, and multi-day shoot schedules that require 2 to 3 days of onsite coverage instead of a single 10-hour day.

The couples who book destination wedding films typically plan weddings with budgets of $200K to $500K+, guest counts of 80 to 150, and venue contracts at properties like Borgo Egnazia in Puglia, Belmond Hotel Caruso in Ravello, Le Sirenuse in Positano, Villa Cetinale in Tuscany, Aman Tokyo, or Hotel du Cap-Eden-Roc in Antibes — venues where the property's architecture, natural light, and cultural context shape the film as much as the ceremony itself.

A destination wedding film from LifeStory.Film includes:

1. **Pre-wedding venue reconnaissance** — site visit or detailed venue coordination call with the local planner to map ceremony and reception locations, identify natural-light windows, and confirm power access.
2. **Multi-day coverage** — typically 2 to 3 days onsite (welcome dinner, wedding day, post-wedding brunch or couple's session).
3. **2-shooter video team** — lead cinematographer and second shooter, both traveling from Los Angeles.
4. **Dual-camera 4K capture** — Sony FX6 and FX3 primary cameras, with backup bodies and lens sets packed in checked and carry-on cases.
5. **Full ceremony and vows coverage** — continuous multi-angle capture of the entire ceremony, not just highlights.
6. **Reception and toast coverage** — parent speeches, first dance, cake cutting, and last dance under string lights or villa courtyard.
7. **8- to 12-week edit turnaround** — same post-production timeline as California weddings.
8. **Highlight reel (4–7 minutes) plus full ceremony edit** — two deliverables, both color-graded and audio-mixed.

Travel, lodging, and meals for the 2-shooter team are billed separately as a flat travel fee, quoted per destination after the venue is confirmed.

## Italy Destination Wedding Film Coverage — Amalfi Coast, Puglia, and Tuscany

![Charming cliffside houses in a coastal Italian town bathed in warm sunlight.](/blog-images/destination-wedding-videographer/section-2-1ea0e7743e37.jpg)

*Photo by Alina Chernii on Pexels*


Italy accounts for roughly 40 percent of the destination wedding inquiries LifeStory.Film receives, with the Amalfi Coast (Ravello, Positano, Praiano), Puglia (Borgo Egnazia, Masseria Torre Maizza), and Tuscany (Villa Cetinale, Castello di Vicarello) representing the three primary regions.

### Amalfi Coast — Ravello and Positano

Belmond Hotel Caruso in Ravello and Le Sirenuse in Positano are the two venues most frequently named in Amalfi Coast wedding inquiries. Both properties offer terraced ceremony sites with Mediterranean views, but the light and logistics differ significantly.

**Belmond Hotel Caruso** sits 350 meters above sea level on a cliff terrace overlooking the Bay of Salerno. Ceremony coverage at Caruso requires planning for wind — the terrace is exposed, and lapel microphones pick up constant wind noise that requires post-production noise reduction. The best ceremony time is 5:00 PM to 6:00 PM, when the sun moves behind the hotel and the terrace shifts into open shade, eliminating harsh facial shadows and reducing wind slightly as temperatures drop.

The infinity pool terrace at Caruso films beautifully during the couple's portrait session immediately after the ceremony — the water reflects the golden-hour sky, and the stone balustrade frames the coastline. Coverage requires 20 to 30 minutes on the pool terrace before guests arrive for cocktail hour.

**Le Sirenuse** in Positano offers a rooftop terrace ceremony with views of the Positano cliffside and the Tyrrhenian Sea. The terrace is smaller than Caruso's — maximum 80 guests — and the ceremony typically takes place at sunset (7:30 PM in July and August, 6:00 PM in September and October). The challenge at Le Sirenuse is color temperature: the terrace receives direct sunlight until the sun drops behind the mountain, at which point the light shifts rapidly from golden to blue. A destination wedding videographer working at Le Sirenuse shoots the ceremony with dual color-temperature presets — 5600K for the sunlit portion, 4500K for the post-sunset blue hour — and matches them in post.

Reception coverage at Le Sirenuse takes place on the same rooftop terrace or in the Franco Bar one level below. The bar's interior is lit by candlelight and vintage sconces, requiring ISO 6400 to ISO 12800 for handheld reception footage without artificial video lighting.

### Puglia — Borgo Egnazia

Borgo Egnazia is a 40-acre resort in Savelletri di Fasano designed to resemble a traditional Puglian village. Weddings at Borgo Egnazia typically include a Catholic or symbolic ceremony in the on-property San Domenico Chapel (a reconstructed 15th-century chapel moved stone by stone to the resort), followed by a cocktail reception in the Piazza and a seated dinner in the Corte or La Frasca courtyard.

The San Domenico Chapel is small — seating for 60 guests maximum — and dark. Natural light enters through a single oculus window above the altar and through the entrance doors. A videographer filming a ceremony at San Domenico needs fast lenses (f/1.4 or f/1.8) and a camera body with strong low-light performance (Sony FX6, Canon C70, or equivalent). The couple processes down the center aisle from the entrance, backlit by the doorway, which creates a natural silhouette effect if the exposure is set for the couple's faces rather than the blown-out background.

Reception coverage in the Corte courtyard benefits from the property's use of warm Edison-bulb string lights and candle-lit long tables. The courtyard's white stone walls reflect the candlelight, providing enough ambient light for handheld B-roll at ISO 3200 without additional lighting.

### Tuscany — Villa Cetinale

Villa Cetinale is a 17th-century villa in the Tuscan countryside near Siena, available for exclusive-use weddings with guest counts of 40 to 100. The property includes a central villa, formal Italian gardens, a cypress alley, a chapel, and a limonaia (lemon house) used for receptions.

Ceremony coverage at Villa Cetinale most often takes place in the chapel or on the front lawn facing the villa façade. The chapel is naturally lit by tall arched windows, and the afternoon light (3:00 PM to 5:00 PM) produces a soft directional glow on the altar. The front lawn ceremony option films better during golden hour (6:00 PM to 7:30 PM in summer), with the villa and cypress alley in the background.

The couple's portrait session at Villa Cetinale typically moves through three locations: the cypress alley (a 300-meter tree-lined path leading up a hill), the formal garden with its geometric boxwood hedges, and the villa's stone terrace. Coverage requires 45 to 60 minutes to complete all three, and the sequence works best immediately after the ceremony while the light remains even.

## France Destination Wedding Film Coverage — Côte d'Azur and Provence

![A picturesque wedding arch adorned with flowers in a lavender field under a clear blue sky.](/blog-images/destination-wedding-videographer/section-3-5682ade724d7.jpg)

*Photo by Edo Rossi on Pexels*


French destination weddings filmed by LifeStory.Film concentrate on two regions: the Côte d'Azur (Antibes, Cannes, Saint-Jean-Cap-Ferrat, Èze) and Provence (Gordes, Ménerbes, Avignon). The most frequently named venue in inquiries is Hotel du Cap-Eden-Roc in Antibes.

**Hotel du Cap-Eden-Roc** is a 22-acre estate on the Cap d'Antibes peninsula with gardens, a cliffside ceremony terrace, and a private beach club. Weddings at Eden-Roc are typically planned as multi-day events — a welcome dinner at the Eden-Roc Beach Club on Friday, the ceremony and reception on the Grand Lawn on Saturday, and a farewell brunch on Sunday.

Ceremony coverage on the Grand Lawn requires planning for Mediterranean wind and full sun. The lawn sits on a bluff 30 meters above the sea, and the ceremony typically takes place at 5:00 PM to 6:00 PM to avoid midday heat for the 100 to 150 guests. The Grand Lawn has no natural shade, so the couple often installs a floral chuppah or fabric canopy, which provides structure for wide ceremony shots and frames the Mediterranean in the background.

The couple's portrait session at Eden-Roc moves to the garden staircase and the stone terrace overlooking the water, both of which require 20 to 30 minutes and benefit from the soft light between 6:30 PM and 7:30 PM after the ceremony.

Reception coverage at Eden-Roc takes place either on the Grand Lawn under a sailcloth tent or inside the hotel's Salon Roses. The outdoor reception option films with natural and string-light sources; the indoor option requires coordination with the property's lighting designer to confirm uplighting and pin-spot timing.

## Japan Destination Wedding Film Coverage — Tokyo and Kyoto

![Sensoji Temple in Tokyo, showcasing traditional architecture and cultural heritage in a vibrant urban setting.](/blog-images/destination-wedding-videographer/section-4-cbb8eabb6996.jpg)

*Photo by Long Nguyen on Pexels*


Japanese destination weddings filmed by LifeStory.Film are typically small (20 to 60 guests), with ceremonies at Shinto shrines or luxury hotels and receptions at kaiseki restaurants, ryokan properties, or hotel ballrooms. The two cities that appear most frequently in inquiries are Tokyo (Aman Tokyo, Palace Hotel Tokyo, Park Hyatt Tokyo) and Kyoto (traditional machiya venues, Nanzen-ji Temple, or private tea houses).

**Aman Tokyo** hosts intimate weddings with Western-style ceremonies in the hotel's 33rd-floor event space overlooking the Imperial Palace Gardens. The floor-to-ceiling windows provide expansive city views but create exposure challenges — the couple is backlit by the windows during daytime ceremonies, requiring careful exposure balancing and fill lighting. Couples who prioritize natural light typically schedule the ceremony for late afternoon (4:00 PM to 5:00 PM) when the sun is lower and the contrast between the couple and the window light is less extreme.

Reception coverage at Aman Tokyo takes place in the same event space or in one of the private dining rooms. The design is minimalist — dark wood, low seating, stone accents — and the lighting is dim, requiring ISO 3200 to ISO 6400 for handheld footage.

**Kyoto Shinto shrine ceremonies** — often at Yasaka Shrine or Shimogamo Shrine — follow traditional Shinto wedding rituals (san-san-kudo sake exchange, tamagushi offering, ritual music by shrine priests). These ceremonies are quiet, formal, and restricted in terms of photographer and videographer movement. Coverage requires advance coordination with the shrine's office to confirm permissible camera positions, audio-recording policies, and movement restrictions during the ritual.

A destination wedding videographer filming a Shinto ceremony typically uses two locked-off cameras on tripods (one wide angle at the rear of the hall, one medium shot from the side) and a third handheld camera for detail shots during moments when movement is allowed. Flash photography is prohibited, so all footage is captured with natural light and the shrine's interior lanterns, requiring ISO 1600 to ISO 6400.

## How We Plan and Confirm Destination Wedding Coverage

1. **Inquiry and venue confirmation** — the couple submits an inquiry form naming the destination venue, wedding date, estimated guest count, and coverage scope (single-day vs. multi-day). If the venue is not yet confirmed, we provide a preliminary quote with a travel-fee range.

2. **Venue reconnaissance and coordination call** — after the venue is confirmed, we schedule a 30- to 45-minute video call with the couple and their planner to review the timeline, ceremony and reception locations, natural-light conditions, vendor load-in and power access, and any venue-specific restrictions (e.g., no drones, no flash photography, no videographer movement during the ceremony).

3. **Contract and deposit** — the contract includes the base wedding film package, the travel fee (flat rate covering airfare, lodging, and meals for the 2-shooter team), and the multi-day coverage scope. A 50 percent deposit is due at signing; the remaining 50 percent is due 30 days before the wedding.

4. **Gear and travel logistics** — we pack dual camera bodies, lenses, audio gear, batteries, and chargers in checked and carry-on cases, with international power adapters and customs carnet documentation (required for professional video gear entering the EU and Japan). We arrive at the destination 2 days before the wedding to acclimate, scout the venue in person, and confirm the timeline with the planner.

5. **Coverage execution** — we shoot the welcome dinner (if included), the wedding day (getting ready, ceremony, portraits, reception), and the post-wedding session (if included), following the same coverage model and shot list used for California weddings at Hotel Bel-Air or San Ysidro Ranch.

6. **Edit and delivery** — the footage is backed up onsite each evening, then transferred to our Los Angeles editing studio within 3 days of the wedding. The edit timeline is 8 to 12 weeks from the wedding date, identical to our California wedding turnaround. The couple receives the highlight reel (4–7 minutes), the full ceremony edit (uncut, multi-angle), and all vows audio as individual files, delivered via private Vimeo link and USB drive.

## What the Travel Fee Covers and How It Is Quoted

The travel fee is a flat rate added to the base wedding film package and varies by destination. It covers:

- Round-trip airfare for 2 team members (lead cinematographer and second shooter) from Los Angeles to the destination
- Lodging for 2 to 4 nights (depending on whether the coverage includes a welcome dinner or post-wedding session)
- Meals and ground transportation during the coverage period
- Excess baggage fees for checked camera cases
- Travel insurance for gear and personnel

The travel fee does NOT cover accommodations or meals for the couple's guests, planner, or other vendors — only the LifeStory.Film video team.

Travel fee ranges by region (2025–2026 estimates):

- **Italy (Amalfi Coast, Puglia, Tuscany):** $3,200 to $4,800
- **France (Côte d'Azur, Provence, Paris):** $3,000 to $4,500
- **Japan (Tokyo, Kyoto):** $4,500 to $6,000
- **Mexico (Cabo, Riviera Maya):** $2,200 to $3,200
- **Caribbean (Turks and Caicos, St. Barts, Jamaica):** $2,800 to $4,000

The exact travel fee is quoted after the venue and timeline are confirmed, typically within 48 hours of the initial inquiry.

## Destination Wedding Film Deliverables and Turnaround

The deliverable package for a destination wedding film matches the California wedding film package:

- **Highlight reel (4–7 minutes)** — a cinematic edit of the full wedding day, color-graded and audio-mixed, with licensed music and vows excerpts woven through the narrative.
- **Full ceremony edit (15–45 minutes)** — the complete ceremony from processional through recessional, captured from 2 camera angles and edited as a continuous multi-angle cut.
- **Vows audio files** — individual WAV files of the couple's spoken vows, extracted from the ceremony audio and delivered separately for keepsake purposes.
- **Reception toast highlights** — edited segments of parent speeches, best-man and maid-of-honor toasts, and any additional speeches, delivered as part of the highlight reel or as a standalone toast reel (if requested).

The edit turnaround is 8 to 12 weeks from the wedding date, regardless of whether the wedding took place in Positano or Ojai. Footage is edited in Los Angeles by the same editing team that works on all LifeStory.Film weddings, ensuring consistent color grading, pacing, and audio mixing.

1. **Couples planning from California** — they live in Los Angeles, Orange County, or the Bay Area, discovered LifeStory.Film through a California wedding search or referral, reviewed the portfolio on the website, and preferred to work with a known team rather than sourcing a local videographer in the destination country.

In all three cases, the decision to hire a Los Angeles–based destination wedding videographer is driven by stylistic consistency, portfolio trust, and communication ease (same time zone for planning calls, same language, same contract and payment structure as a California wedding).

## Multi-Day Destination Wedding Coverage — Welcome Dinner, Wedding Day, and Post-Wedding Session

Roughly 60 percent of destination wedding inquiries include multi-day coverage rather than wedding-day-only coverage. The most common multi-day structure is:

- **Day 1 (Friday):** Welcome dinner — 2 to 3 hours of coverage at a rehearsal dinner, welcome cocktail party, or casual group dinner at a local restaurant or villa terrace. Deliverable: 2- to 3-minute welcome-dinner highlight reel or inclusion in the main wedding film.
- **Day 2 (Saturday):** Wedding day — full-day coverage from getting-ready through reception departure (10 to 12 hours). Deliverable: 4- to 7-minute highlight reel plus full ceremony edit.
- **Day 3 (Sunday):** Post-wedding couple's session — 1 to 2 hours of portrait coverage at a scenic location near the venue (the Amalfi Coast Path, a Tuscan vineyard, the Imperial Palace Gardens in Tokyo). Deliverable: 1- to 2-minute couple's portrait film or inclusion in the main wedding film.

The multi-day package adds 4 to 6 hours of coverage to the base wedding film package and extends the travel fee by one additional night of lodging and meals.
